# Marketing strategy hypotheses

All experiments are **PROPOSED — NOT EXECUTED**. There are no observed uplift, sales, acquisition-cost or conversion results. The customer segments and channels below are hypotheses derived from product-offer differences, not measured consumer segmentation.

## M1 — Make carried-weight trade-offs legible
**Objective:** Improve informed product consideration.
**Target customer hypothesis:** Budget-conscious beginner backpackers weighing the Cloud Up upgrade.
**Rationale/evidence:** I1 in BUSINESS_FINDINGS.md: 20 g reported minimum-weight difference versus 270 g packed-weight difference; \$40 display-price increment. Sources: nh-cloud-up-20d-source and nh-cloud-up-pro-standard-source.
**Hypothesis:** A card showing both weight definitions and price improves specification comprehension and qualified product visits relative to lifestyle copy.
**Proposed channel:** An owned comparison landing page reached by an explicitly authorized, separately budgeted acquisition test.
**Experiment design:** Randomize visitors 1:1 to specification card or lifestyle copy; identical products, CTA, geography, availability and delivery experience. Fix eligibility, duration, analysis method and minimum practical effect before launch. Exclude automated visits consistently; analyze intention-to-treat. Obtain traffic baseline first; no sample-size claim exists yet.
**Measurement:** Primary = unique eligible visitors clicking product detail / unique eligible exposed visitors. Secondary = comprehension-task accuracy, engaged visits and exit rate. Record allocation and instrumentation quality before interpreting differences.
**Constraints:** No site access, traffic baseline, budget or launch authorization; the base option may be unavailable. A real experiment needs a currently purchasable offer and consent-appropriate instrumentation.
**Decision criterion:** Adopt only if the predeclared practical CTR improvement and comprehension guardrail are met with valid allocation; otherwise retain or revise copy. Numerical thresholds and sample size remain pending real baseline data.

## M2 — Explain product attributes behind price
**Objective:** Help shoppers choose an offer appropriate to their use.
**Target customer hypothesis:** Two-person weekend hikers comparing interior access, floor space and carried weight.
**Rationale/evidence:** I2: sampled REI products share floor area, door count and footprint inclusion but differ in price and weight definitions. This shows that three features alone cannot explain premiums; it does not show Naturehike superiority.
**Hypothesis:** A feature-specific comparison improves qualified consideration over an undifferentiated "best value" message.
**Proposed channel:** Owned educational content; search acquisition is a candidate to validate after real demand research.
**Experiment design:** Randomize eligible visitors between a source-linked feature comparison and a generic value card. Keep source date and comparison scope visible; do not imply competitor quality failures. Use the same prices and available products in each arm.
**Measurement:** Primary = comparison-page visitors clicking a relevant product / exposed eligible visitors. Secondary = feature comprehension, return visits and relevance feedback. Future add-to-cart rate is permissible only with authorized real storefront analytics.
**Constraints:** Convenience product sample, missing material controls, no search-demand export and no customer preference study.
**Decision criterion:** Predeclare a practical engagement threshold after baseline collection; require no decline in comprehension or inaccurate-claim complaints. No outcome is promised.

## M3 — Clarify included accessories
**Objective:** Reduce uncertainty about what the listed offer includes.
**Target customer hypothesis:** First-time backpackers unfamiliar with footprints and packed-weight contents.
**Rationale/evidence:** I3: verified footprint flags vary between Naturehike/REI bundled offers and Kelty Late Start. Shipping and complete-kit costs remain unverified.
**Hypothesis:** An included/optional equipment checklist improves informed engagement and reduces accessory clarification needs.
**Proposed channel:** Owned product-detail educational module or an authorized FAQ test.
**Experiment design:** Randomize visitors between a concise checklist and current description; hold price, stock and CTA constant. Keep optional items optional. Pilot comprehension interviews before a live traffic test; no participants have been recruited.
**Measurement:** Primary = correctly answered inclusion questions / respondents completing the task. Secondary = product-detail engagement and clarification-contact rate where authorized data exist.
**Constraints:** No survey participants, support logs or conversion data; inclusion alone may have little value to experienced owners.
**Decision criterion:** Require predeclared comprehension improvement with no misleading total-cost impression; determine the numerical threshold and participant sample before recruitment.
